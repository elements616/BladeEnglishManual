    1. Service Area

      1. There is a considerable difference in the size of the service areas of AM and FM monaural, and even when AM can be received clearly, FM stereo broadcasts may not be receivable. Also, FM stereo not only has the smallest service area but is also susceptible to noise and other interference ("fading noise", "multipath noise", "fade out").



      1. Reception Problems

        □ HINT □
        In addition to static problems, there are also problems called "fading noise", "multipath noise" and "fade out". These problems are not caused by electrical noise but by the nature of the radio waves themselves.




        1. Fading Noise


          1. In addition to electrical interference, AM broadcasts are susceptible to other interference, especially at night. This occurs because AM radio waves are reflected by the ionosphere at night, and the reflected waves interfere with the waves from the same station that reach the vehicle antenna directly. This interference is called "fading noise".




        2. Multipath Noise


          1. Interference caused by radio waves reflecting off obstacles is called "multipath". Multipath occurs when signals from the broadcast station antenna are reflected off buildings or mountains and interfere with the directly received signals. This interference is called "multipath noise".




        3. Fade Out


          1. FM radio waves have a higher frequency than AM radio waves, so they are reflected by obstacles such as buildings and mountains. For this reason, when the vehicle is in the shadow of buildings or other obstacles, the FM signal gradually fades or weakens. This phenomenon is called "fade out".

          2. Points to Note When Checking


            1. Noise entering the radio is suppressed, so it is normally not harmful in actual use, and very loud noise is rarely heard. If very loud noise enters the radio, check that the ground at the area where the antenna is installed is normal.

            2. Check that all genuine noise suppression parts are correctly installed, and that no aftermarket parts or non-genuine wiring have been installed.

            3. If the radio is not tuned to a channel (not tuned to a broadcast), noise becomes prominent, making it easier to judge the symptom.




          3. Antenna and Noise


            1. Electrical signals received by the antenna are transmitted to the radio through the core wire of the coaxial cable. Noise waves other than radio waves enter this core wire, causing noise in the radio and degrading sound quality. To prevent noise from entering the radio, the core wire in the coaxial cable is covered with a mesh wire called the shield wire. This shield wire prevents noise from entering by conducting the noise to ground.

              □ HINT □
              If the ground of this shield wire is defective, noise will occur.



          1. CD


            1. Usable Discs




              1. This player can only use music CDs, CD-Rs (CD-Recordable) and CD-RWs (CD-ReWritable) bearing the marks shown in the illustration.



            2. Precautions for Disc Use

              ■ NOTICE ■
              1. Copy control CDs cannot be used.
              2. DualDiscs (CD and DVD bonded back to back) cannot be used.
              3. DTS-CDs cannot be used.
              4. CD-TEXT can be used.
              5. CD-Rs and CD-RWs may not be playable due to recording conditions, disc characteristics, scratches, dirt or deterioration from long exposure to the vehicle interior environment.
              6. CD-Rs and CD-RWs that have not been finalized cannot be played.
              7. Handle discs so as not to leave fingerprints, dirt or scratches on them.
              8. When handling a disc, hold it by the center hole and edge with the label side up.
              9. If the disc eject button is pressed and the disc is left ejected for a long time, the CD may deform and become unusable.
              10. If a disc with cellophane tape, stickers, CD-R labels, rental CD labels, etc. attached, or with traces of their removal, is used, the disc may not eject or the device may malfunction.
              11. Store discs away from direct sunlight. (The disc may deform and become unusable)
              12. Discs that are not circular with a diameter of 12cm or 8cm cannot be played.
              13. Do not use specially shaped discs, as they may cause the device to malfunction.
              14. Do not use discs with transparent or translucent sections in the recording area, as they may not load, eject or play normally.
              15. No adapter is required when playing a single CD (8cm).

              □ HINT □
              1. In cold or rainy weather, condensation (water droplets) may form inside the player, just as the glass inside the vehicle fogs up. In this case, sound may skip or playback may stop, so ventilate or dehumidify for a while before use.
              2. If the player vibrates severely, such as when driving on rough roads, sound may skip.




            3. Cleaning

              ■ NOTICE ■
              Do not use a lens cleaner, as it may cause the player's pickup section to malfunction.


              1. Clean dirt off discs by wiping lightly in a radial direction from the center outward with a soft, dry cloth such as a cleaning cloth for plastic eyeglass lenses.

                ■ NOTICE ■
                1. Pressing hard on a disc by hand or rubbing it with a hard cloth may scratch the surface.
                2. Using solvents such as record spray, antistatic agents, alcohol, benzine or thinner, or chemically treated cloths, may damage the disc and make it unusable.

                1. AVC-LAN Communication System

                  1. The audio system communicates between devices via AVC-LAN only when dealer-installed devices are connected.

                  2. The master device of the AVC-LAN is the radio receiver assembly.

                  3. The radio receiver assembly contains a resistor necessary for communication transmission (60 to 80 Ω between terminals TX+ and TX-).

                  4. If a malfunction such as a short between communication lines, short to +B or short to ground occurs in the AVC-LAN circuit, communication is interrupted and the audio system does not operate properly.



                  1. Diagnosis Function

                    1. The audio system has a diagnostic function (displayed on the LCD of the radio receiver assembly)

                    2. Each device constituting the AVC-LAN is assigned a 3-digit number (expressed in base 16) called the device code (physical address).

                    3. Each function (unit) within the AVC-LAN is assigned a 2-digit number (expressed in base 16) called the logical address.
