Circuit Description


A three-way catalytic converter is used to efficiently purify the CO (carbon monoxide), HC (hydrocarbons) and NOx (nitrogen oxides) in the exhaust gas. As the three-way catalyst functions most efficiently near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio, CO, HC and NOx cannot be purified efficiently unless the engine is accurately controlled near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio. The air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) is installed for the purpose of this control. The air fuel ratio sensor has the characteristic of outputting a voltage approximately proportional to the air-fuel ratio, and the engine control computer controls the air-fuel ratio based on this voltage.

A zirconium element is used for the sensor element. The inside of the zirconium element is exposed to the outside air and the outside is exposed to the exhaust gas, and it generates a voltage according to the difference in oxygen concentration between the exhaust gas and the outside air. When the exhaust temperature is low, the sensor does not generate a useful voltage signal without supplemental heating. For this reason, the air fuel ratio sensor has a heater that warms the zirconia element, and the engine control computer regulates the heater current by duty ratio control.




DTC Detection Condition
  1. During air-fuel ratio feedback, if the air fuel ratio sensor continues to output lean even though the oxygen sensor is outputting rich, the engine control computer sets DTC P2195/28 or P2197/21 and illuminates the check engine warning light.
    Also, during air-fuel ratio feedback, if the air fuel ratio sensor continues to output rich even though the oxygen sensor is outputting lean, the engine control computer sets DTC P2196/28 or P2198/21 and illuminates the check engine warning light.
  2. When the air fuel ratio sensor current during fuel cut is approximately 3.6 mA or more (in this case P2195/28, P2197/21) or approximately 1.4 mA or less (in this case P2196/28, P2198/21), the engine control computer sets a DTC and illuminates the check engine warning light.

Function Description


Confirmation Driving
By performing confirmation driving, it is possible to check whether the DTC is reproduced.







  1. Connect SST (TaSCAN) to DLC3.
  2. Turn IG ON.
  3. Turn the main switch of SST (TaSCAN) ON.
  4. Using SST (TaSCAN), clear the DTCs.
  5. Start the engine, turn all accessory switches OFF, and warm up the engine until the engine coolant temperature stabilizes. (A)
  6. Following the SST (TaSCAN) screen display, select [ECU Data Monitor] - [F/C at Idle ON].
  7. Drive the vehicle at 75 to 80 km/h for approximately 2 minutes. (B)
  8. Move the shift position to 2 gear. (C)
  9. Drive the vehicle at a speed suitable for fuel cut. (For details, see the hint below)(D)

    □ HINT □
    Fuel cut is performed under the following conditions.
    1. Release the accelerator pedal.
    2. Fuel injection resumes at 1200r/min

  10. Drive the vehicle with the accelerator pedal depressed at a speed of 65km/h or more for 10 seconds or more. (E)
  11. Immediately after performing step (E), release the accelerator for 4 seconds or more to perform fuel cut. (Do not depress the brake pedal at this time)(F)
  12. Decelerate the vehicle until it reaches 10km/h.
  13. Repeat steps (C) through (E) 3 times or more.

    ■ WARNING ■
    When performing this driving test, be sure to obey speed limits and other traffic laws.


□ HINT □
If there is a malfunction, the check engine warning light comes on during step (B).



Inspection Procedure


□ HINT □
The malfunctioning area can be identified by performing the "Injection Volume" active test. This active test can decrease the fuel injection volume by -12.5 % or increase it by 25 %.


  1. Connect SST (TaSCAN) to DLC3.
  2. Turn IG ON.
  3. Turn the main switch of SST (TaSCAN) ON.
  4. Start the engine, turn all accessory switches OFF, and warm up the engine until the engine coolant temperature stabilizes.
  5. While depressing the accelerator pedal 60 % or more, keep the engine speed at 2500r/min for approximately 3 minutes to warm up each sensor.
  6. Run the engine at idle, and read the output voltages of the air fuel ratio sensor and oxygen sensor when the fuel injection volume is increased and decreased using the "Injection Volume" active test.

    Result
    The output voltages of the air fuel ratio sensor and oxygen sensor change according to the increase and decrease in fuel injection volume.

    Fuel Injection Volume
    		Air fuel ratio sensor output voltage
    		Oxygen sensor output voltage
    +25 % increase
    		Less than 3.0 V (rich condition)
    		0.55 V or more (rich condition)
    -12.5 % decrease
    		3.35 V or more (lean condition)
    		Less than 0.4 V (lean condition)

    ■ NOTICE ■
    The air fuel ratio sensor output voltage has a delay of a few seconds, and the oxygen sensor output has a delay of up to approximately 20 seconds.

Step 2Read TaSCAN Data (A/F Sensor Voltage B1S1,A/F Sensor Voltage B2S1)
SST
09991-70200  

  1. Connect SST (TaSCAN) to DLC3.

  1. Turn IG ON.

  1. Turn the main switch of SST (TaSCAN) ON.

  1. Start the engine, turn all accessory switches OFF, and warm up the engine until the engine coolant temperature stabilizes.

  1. Depress the accelerator pedal and hold at 2500 r/min for approximately 3 minutes to warm up the air fuel ratio sensor.

  1. Allow the engine to idle.

  1. Following the SST (TaSCAN) screen display, read [ECU Data Monitor] - [A/F Sensor Voltage B1S1 / A/F Sensor Voltage B2S1], and check that the air fuel ratio sensor output voltage is normal (varies around 3.3 V).

    ■ NOTICE ■
    Perform this immediately after the sensor warm-up procedure, as the air fuel ratio sensor will cool down.

  1. Cancel the maintenance mode and move the shift position to "B".



  1. Accelerate to 60 km/h and check that the output voltage of the air fuel ratio sensor rises (lean condition) when the accelerator pedal is released (during deceleration fuel cut).

    1. If the air fuel ratio sensor output is fixed at 3.3 V, the circuit may be open.
    2. If the air fuel ratio sensor output is fixed at 3.8 V or more or 2.8 V or less, the circuit may be shorted.
    3. When the engine control computer performs deceleration fuel cut, the air-fuel ratio becomes lean and the air fuel ratio sensor voltage rises.
    4. The engine control computer needs to learn the throttle position fully closed position for fuel cut. If the battery terminal has been disconnected, the learned value is canceled, so it is necessary to drive at 16 km/h or more to perform learning.
    5. During acceleration, etc., the engine control computer temporarily increases the fuel amount, so the air fuel ratio sensor output voltage may be 2.8 V or less.
    6. The air fuel ratio sensor uses a current detection element, so there is no voltage change even if the engine control computer terminal voltage is measured. The voltage displayed on SST (TaSCAN) is the current output by the air fuel ratio sensor converted into voltage.
