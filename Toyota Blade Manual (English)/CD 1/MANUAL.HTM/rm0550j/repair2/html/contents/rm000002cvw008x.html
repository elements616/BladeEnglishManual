Circuit Description



  1. These DTCs are stored when an open circuit, short circuit or output drop of the A/F sensor (air fuel ratio sensor) is detected. To detect these, the engine control computer checks the A/F sensor voltage with the IG ON, and the admittance (ease of current flow) while the engine is running. Normally, the A/F sensor voltage is between 0.6V and 4.4V; a malfunction is determined if it is outside this range or if the admittance is lower than the standard value. If the same malfunction is detected during continuous operation, the warning light comes on and a DTC is stored. The A/F sensor consists of an element made of various metals and a heater for activating the element, and is located between the exhaust manifold and catalytic converter. The element outputs the oxygen concentration in the exhaust gas as a voltage. The engine control computer calculates the air-fuel ratio based on this voltage and adjusts the fuel injection volume according to the air-fuel ratio and engine conditions. While the engine is running, the A/F sensor voltage varies between 0.6V and 4.4V; the voltage rises when there is more oxygen in the exhaust gas (air-fuel ratio lean) and falls when there is less oxygen in the exhaust gas (air-fuel ratio rich). The heater heats the element according to engine conditions to activate it. Battery voltage is applied to the heater, which is controlled by a duty signal.
