DTC P2A00/21 A/F Sensor B1S1 Response Deterioration

Circuit Description


For the procedure, seefor reference.

DTC Detection Condition
  1. The air fuel ratio sensor outputs a voltage approximately proportional to the air-fuel ratio. Based on this output voltage, the engine control computer determines whether the air-fuel ratio is rich or lean and performs feedback control.
    If the engine control computer determines that the response of the air fuel ratio sensor to changes in the air-fuel ratio is slower than the threshold value, it sets a DTC and illuminates the check engine warning light.


DTC No.
SAE/TCCS
P2A00/21
DTC Detection Condition
Diagnosis Condition
When all of the following conditions are met
  1. Air-fuel ratio feedback control in progress
  2. Air fuel ratio sensor activated
  3. Engine speed is less than 4000 r/min
  4. Engine load is 10 to 70%
  5. Shift position is 2th gear or higher
  6. Intake air amount is 7.7 to 38 g/sec
  7. Engine coolant temperature is 80°C or higher
Abnormal Condition
Air fuel ratio sensor response is slower than the threshold value
Malfunction Duration
10 seconds or more
Trip Count
2 trip
Detection Method
Detected 1 time(s) during 1 trip(s). (No detection is performed after determination)
Sensors Used for Detection
Main
  1. Air Fuel Ratio Sensor
Sub
  1. Speed Sensor
  2. Crankshaft Position Sensor
  3. Intake Air Flow Meter Sub-assembly
  4. E.F.I. water temperature sensor
Inspection Area
  1. Wire Harness or Connector
  2. Air Fuel Ratio Sensor
  3. Intake System
  4. Fuel pressure
  5. Fuel Injector Assembly
  6. PCV hose connection condition
  7. Engine Control Computer

After the engine is warmed up, the engine control computer performs air-fuel ratio feedback control to maintain the air-fuel ratio at the stoichiometric level. In addition, after the preconditions are met, active air-fuel ratio control is performed for approximately 30 seconds or more to measure the air fuel ratio sensor response rate. During active air-fuel ratio control, the engine control computer forcibly increases and decreases the injection volume by a fixed amount based on the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio learned during normal air-fuel ratio control, and measures the response rate of the air fuel ratio sensor. The engine control computer receives a signal from the air fuel ratio sensor during active air-fuel ratio control, and uses this signal to calculate the response rate level of the air fuel ratio sensor. If the air fuel ratio sensor response rate level is less than the threshold value, the engine control computer interprets this as a malfunction, sets a DTC and illuminates the check warning light.

□ HINT □
  1. If the air fuel ratio sensor output voltage remains at approximately 3.3 V in any of the conditions, including the above condition (see the malfunction case in the illustration), the air fuel ratio sensor may have an open circuit. (This also occurs when the air fuel ratio sensor heater has an open circuit.)
  2. If the air fuel ratio sensor output voltage remains at approximately 3.8 V or more, or 2.8 V or less in any of the conditions, the air fuel ratio sensor may have a short circuit.
  3. The engine control computer stops fuel injection during engine deceleration (fuel cut). This causes a lean condition and results in a momentary increase in the air fuel ratio sensor output voltage.
  4. While driving the vehicle:
    The air fuel ratio sensor output voltage may fall below 2.8 V during fuel enrichment. For the vehicle, this corresponds to sudden acceleration with the accelerator pedal fully depressed, such as when overtaking. The air fuel ratio sensor is functioning normally.
  5. The air fuel ratio sensor is a current output element. Therefore, the current is converted into voltage inside the engine control computer. Measuring the voltage at the air fuel ratio sensor connector or engine control computer connector will show a constant voltage result.
