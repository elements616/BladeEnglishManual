Circuit Description


A three-way catalytic converter is used to efficiently purify the CO, HC and NOx in the exhaust gas. This three-way catalytic converter functions most efficiently near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio. For this reason, CO, HC and NOx cannot be efficiently purified unless the engine is accurately controlled near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio. The oxygen sensor is installed for the purpose of this control. Using the characteristic that its output voltage changes suddenly when it detects the oxygen concentration in the exhaust gas emitted when normal combustion takes place near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio, the sensor sends signals to the engine control computer so that air-fuel ratio control can be performed accurately. When the air-fuel ratio becomes lean, the oxygen concentration in the exhaust gas increases, and the oxygen sensor informs the engine control computer that the air-fuel ratio is lean. (Low electromotive force: <0.45V) When the air-fuel ratio becomes rich, the oxygen concentration in the exhaust gas decreases, and the oxygen sensor informs the engine control computer that the air-fuel ratio is rich. (High electromotive force: >0.45V) The engine control computer determines whether the air-fuel ratio is rich or lean from the oxygen sensor electromotive force signal, and controls the injection volume accordingly. A zirconium element is used for the sensor element. The zirconium element is made of platinum-plated zirconia and includes an integrated heating element. The inside of the zirconia element is exposed to outside air and the outside to exhaust gas, and it generates a slight voltage when there is a large difference between the oxygen concentrations of the exhaust gas and the outside air. When heated, the sensor becomes very efficient. When the exhaust temperature is low, the sensor does not generate a useful voltage signal without supplemental heating. For this reason, the oxygen sensor has a heater to warm the zirconium element, and the engine control computer regulates the supplemental heating using a duty cycle approach to adjust the average current in the zirconium element.
