Circuit Description



  1. A three-way catalytic converter is used to efficiently purify the CO, HC and NOx in the exhaust gas. This three-way catalytic converter functions most efficiently near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio. For this reason, CO, HC and NOx cannot be efficiently purified unless the engine is accurately controlled near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio. The oxygen sensor is installed for the purpose of this control. Utilizing the characteristic that its output voltage changes suddenly when it detects the oxygen concentration in the exhaust gas discharged when normal combustion occurs near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio, it sends signals to the engine control computer so that the air-fuel ratio can be accurately controlled. When the air-fuel ratio becomes lean, the oxygen concentration in the exhaust gas increases, and the oxygen sensor informs the engine control computer that the air-fuel ratio is lean. (Low voltage output: <0.45V) When the air-fuel ratio becomes rich, the oxygen concentration in the exhaust gas decreases, and the oxygen sensor informs the engine control computer that the air-fuel ratio is rich. (High voltage output: >0.45V) The engine control computer judges whether the air-fuel ratio is rich or lean based on the electromotive force signal of the oxygen sensor, and controls the injection volume accordingly. A zirconium element is used for the sensor element. The zirconium element is made of platinum-plated zirconia and includes an integrated heating element. The inside of the zirconium element is exposed to the outside air and the outside is exposed to the exhaust gas, and it generates a small voltage when there is a large difference in oxygen concentration between the exhaust gas and the outside air. When heated, the sensor becomes very efficient. When the exhaust temperature is low, the sensor does not generate a useful voltage signal without supplemental heating. For this reason, the oxygen sensor has a heater to warm the zirconium element, and the engine control computer regulates the supplemental heating using a duty cycle approach to adjust the average current in the zirconium element.

Inspection Area
P0136/27
  1. Diagnosis is performed when all of the following conditions are met
    1. After engine warm-up (coolant temperature 95 °C or higher)
    2. Oxygen sensor heater operating time 22 seconds or more
  2. Detected when either of the following conditions is met
    1. No rich signal is input even once during feedback (output voltage never reaches 0.6 V or higher)
    2. No lean signal is input even once during feedback (output voltage never drops below 0.45 V)
  3. 480 seconds

              Step 12Read TaSCAN Data
              SST
              09991-70200  

              1. Start the engine and maintain 2500r/min for 90 seconds to warm up the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor).

              1. Using SST (TaSCAN), check that the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage is normal.

              1. Shift into 1 gear.

              1. Accelerate to a vehicle speed of 60km/h, and check that the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage rises when the accelerator pedal is released (when deceleration fuel cut is performed).

                1. If the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage is fixed at 3.3V, the circuit may be open.
                2. If the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage is 3.8V or more or 2.8V or less, the circuit may be shorted.
                3. When the engine control computer performs deceleration fuel cut, the air-fuel ratio becomes lean and the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) voltage rises.
                4. The engine control computer learns the fully closed throttle position for fuel cut. If the battery terminal is disconnected, the learned value is cancelled, so it is necessary to drive at 16km/h or more to allow it to be learned.
                5. During acceleration, etc., the engine control computer increases the fuel volume, so the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage may be 2.8V or less.

                6. As the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) uses a current detection element, there is no voltage change even if the terminal voltage of the engine control computer is measured. The voltage displayed on SST (TaSCAN) is the current output by the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) converted to voltage.
