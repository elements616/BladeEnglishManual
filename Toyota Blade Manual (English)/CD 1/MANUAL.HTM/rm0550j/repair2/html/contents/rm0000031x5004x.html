Circuit Description





When a misfire occurs in the engine, hydrocarbons (HC) are emitted in the exhaust gas. When the HC concentration rises, exhaust emission levels deteriorate. At the same time, a rise in HC concentration raises the catalyst temperature, which may impair the purification ability of the catalyst. To prevent increased exhaust emissions and thermal deterioration of the catalyst, the engine control computer monitors the misfire rate (number of misfires divided by number of ignitions). The engine control computer uses the camshaft position sensor and crankshaft position sensor to monitor misfires. The crankshaft position sensor is used to measure the crankshaft rotation speed. When the variation in crankshaft rotation speed exceeds the judgment value, the engine control computer determines that a misfire is occurring and increments the misfire counter (cumulative number of misfires).

The camshaft position sensor is used to identify the misfiring cylinder.




When the misfire rate exceeds the judgment value, the engine control computer illuminates or blinks the check engine warning light.

When catalyst overtemperature level misfire is detected

  1. The misfire rate is judged every 200 engine revolutions, and when a misfire rate that causes thermal deterioration of the catalyst (several tens of percent or more) occurs 3 times, the engine control computer immediately blinks the check engine warning light.

When emission deterioration level misfire is detected

  1. The misfire rate is judged every 1000 engine revolutions, and when a misfire rate at which emissions exceed the judgment value (several percent or more) occurs 4 times (on the 2 trip), the engine control computer illuminates the check engine warning light.

Function Description


  1. Connect SST (TaSCAN) to DLC3.
  2. Record the DTCs and freeze frame data.
  3. Following the SST (TaSCAN) display screen, switch from normal mode to check mode.
  4. Following the SST (TaSCAN) display screen, read the misfire counter value of each cylinder while idling.
  5. If the misfire counter values cannot be read in 4., drive the vehicle several times under the conditions recorded in the freeze frame data, such as engine speed and engine load.

    ■ NOTICE ■
    To detect a misfire DTC, drive the vehicle for the time shown in the table below under the conditions recorded in the freeze frame data, such as engine speed and engine load. Do not turn the ignition OFF at this time. Turning the ignition OFF changes the mode from check mode to normal mode and clears all DTCs, freeze frame data, etc.

  6. Following the SST (TaSCAN) display screen, check and record the DTCs, freeze frame data and misfire counter.
  7. Turn the IG OFF and wait for 5 seconds or more.

Inspection Procedure


□ HINT □
  1. If DTCs other than misfire DTCs are stored at the same time, repair those DTCs first.
  2. Using SST (TaSCAN), read the freeze data. Freeze frame data records part of the engine operating condition when the malfunction occurred, and this information is useful when troubleshooting.
  3. If the symptom cannot be confirmed, reproduce the conditions recorded in the freeze frame data.
  4. If either the F/B correction value bank 1 or the F/B learned value bank 1 in the freeze frame data is outside the range of ±20 %, the air-fuel ratio may be rich (-20 % or less) or lean (+20 % or more).
  5. If the engine coolant temperature in the freeze frame data is less than 80 degrees, the misfire may occur only while the engine is warming up.
  6. Possible reasons why a misfire cannot be reproduced include running out of fuel, use of improper fuel and fouled spark plugs.
  7. After repair, check the misfire counter value.
