Circuit Description


A three-way catalyst is used to efficiently purify CO (carbon monoxide), HC (hydrocarbons) and NOx (nitrogen oxides) in the exhaust gas. As the three-way catalyst functions most efficiently near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio, CO, HC and NOx cannot be purified efficiently unless the engine is accurately controlled near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio. The oxygen sensor is installed for the purpose of this control. The oxygen sensor uses the characteristic that its output voltage changes suddenly when it detects the oxygen concentration in exhaust gas discharged under normal combustion near the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio, and sends a signal to the engine control computer so that the air-fuel ratio can be controlled accurately.

A zirconia element is used as the sensor element. The inside of the zirconium element is exposed to the outside air and the outside is exposed to the exhaust gas, and it generates a voltage according to the difference in oxygen concentration between the exhaust gas and the outside air. The engine control computer determines whether the air-fuel ratio is rich or lean based on the output voltage of the oxygen sensor, and controls the injection volume accordingly. When the exhaust temperature is low, the sensor cannot generate a useful voltage signal without supplementary heating. Therefore, the oxygen sensor has a heater that warms the zirconia element, and the engine control computer adjusts the heater current using duty ratio control.




DTC Detection Condition
  1. Active air-fuel ratio control
    While the vehicle is being driven after the engine has warmed up, the engine control computer performs active air-fuel ratio control for approximately 30 seconds or more. At this time, the engine control computer forcibly adjusts the air-fuel ratio to a lean or rich condition. During this time, deterioration of the three-way catalyst and malfunctions of the oxygen sensor are detected.

  2. Oxygen sensor voltage malfunction
    During active air-fuel ratio control, the engine control computer forcibly adjusts the air-fuel ratio to a lean or rich condition. If the oxygen sensor malfunctions, the amount of change in its output voltage becomes small.
    If the oxygen sensor output voltage stays only between 0.21 V and 0.59 V during active air-fuel ratio control, the engine control computer determines that there is an oxygen sensor voltage malfunction and stores P0136/29 or P0156/27. It then illuminates the check engine warning light.




  3. Open or short in oxygen sensor circuit
    During active air-fuel ratio control, the engine control computer forcibly adjusts the air-fuel ratio to a lean or rich condition.
    If the oxygen sensor output voltage is 0.21V or less (lean) even though the active air-fuel ratio control target is rich, the engine control computer determines that there is a malfunction in the oxygen sensor circuit and stores P0137/29 or P0157/27. It then illuminates the check engine warning light.
    If the oxygen sensor output voltage is 0.59V or more (rich) even though the active air-fuel ratio control target is lean, the engine control computer determines that there is a malfunction in the oxygen sensor circuit and stores P0138/29 or P0158/27. It then illuminates the check engine warning light.
    If the oxygen sensor output voltage remains at 1.2V or more for 10 seconds or more, the engine control computer determines that there is a malfunction in the oxygen sensor circuit and stores P0138/29 or P0158/27. It then illuminates the check engine warning light.

Confirmation Driving
By performing confirmation driving, it is possible to check whether the DTC is reproduced.




  1. Connect SST (TaSCAN) to DLC3.
  2. Turn IG ON.
  3. Turn the main switch of SST (TaSCAN) ON.
  4. Using SST (TaSCAN), clear the DTCs.
  5. Start the engine, turn all accessory switches OFF, and warm up the engine until the engine coolant temperature stabilizes.
  6. Drive the vehicle at 75 to 80 km/h for approximately 2 minutes.
  7. Following the SST (TaSCAN) screen display, check the DTC result. (see)


Inspection Procedure


□ HINT □
The malfunctioning area can be identified by performing the "Injection Volume" active test. This active test can decrease the fuel injection volume by -12.5 % or increase it by 25 %.


  1. Connect SST (TaSCAN) to DLC3.
  2. Turn IG ON.
  3. Turn the main switch of SST (TaSCAN) ON.
  4. Start the engine, turn all accessory switches OFF, and warm up the engine until the engine coolant temperature stabilizes.
  5. While depressing the accelerator pedal 60 % or more, keep the engine speed at 2500r/min for approximately 3 minutes to warm up each sensor.
  6. Run the engine at idle, and read the output voltages of the air fuel ratio sensor and oxygen sensor when the fuel injection volume is increased and decreased using the "Injection Volume" active test.

    Result
    The output voltages of the air fuel ratio sensor and oxygen sensor change according to the increase and decrease in fuel injection volume.

    Fuel Injection Volume
    		Air fuel ratio sensor output voltage
    		Oxygen sensor output voltage
    +25 % increase
    		Less than 3.0 V (rich condition)
    		0.55 V or more (rich condition)
    -12.5 % decrease
    		3.35 V or more (lean condition)
    		Less than 0.4 V (lean condition)

    ■ NOTICE ■
    The air fuel ratio sensor output voltage has a delay of a few seconds, and the oxygen sensor output has a delay of up to approximately 20 seconds.
