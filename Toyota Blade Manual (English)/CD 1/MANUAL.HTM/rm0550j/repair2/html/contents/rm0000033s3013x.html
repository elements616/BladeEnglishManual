Preparation List


Circuit Description


For the circuit description,for reference.

DTC Detection Condition
  1. During air-fuel ratio feedback, if the air fuel ratio sensor continues to output lean even though the oxygen sensor is outputting rich, the engine control computer sets DTC P2195/21 and illuminates the check engine warning light.
    Also, during air-fuel ratio feedback, if the air fuel ratio sensor continues to output rich even though the oxygen sensor is outputting lean, the engine control computer sets DTC P2196/21 and illuminates the check engine warning light.
  2. When the air fuel ratio sensor current during fuel cut is approximately 3.6 mA or more (in this case P2195/21) or approximately 1.0 mA or less (in this case P2196/21), the engine control computer sets a DTC and illuminates the check engine warning light.

Diagnosis Condition
P2195/21
  1. Air fuel ratio sensor voltage lean malfunction
    1. 30 seconds or more have elapsed after engine start and air-fuel ratio feedback control is in progress
  1. Air fuel ratio sensor current malfunction
    1. When all of the following conditions are met
      1. Engine coolant temperature is 75°C or higher
      2. Air fuel ratio sensor activated
      3. During fuel cut (6 to 12 seconds)
P2196/21
  1. Air fuel ratio sensor voltage rich malfunction
    1. 30 seconds or more have elapsed after engine start and air-fuel ratio feedback control is in progress
  1. Air fuel ratio sensor current malfunction
    1. When all of the following conditions are met
      1. Engine coolant temperature is 75°C or higher
      2. Air fuel ratio sensor activated
      3. During fuel cut (6 to 12 seconds)
Abnormal Condition
P2195/21
  1. Air fuel ratio sensor voltage lean malfunction
    1. Air fuel ratio sensor output voltage is 3.8 V or more and oxygen sensor is 0.21 V or more
  1. Air fuel ratio sensor current malfunction
    1. Air fuel ratio sensor current is 3.6 mA or more during fuel cut
P2196/21
  1. Air fuel ratio sensor voltage rich malfunction
    1. Air fuel ratio sensor output voltage is 2.8 V or less and oxygen sensor is 0.59 V or less
  1. Air fuel ratio sensor current malfunction
    1. Air fuel ratio sensor current is 1.0 mA or less during fuel cut

Function Description


Confirmation Driving
By performing confirmation driving, it is possible to check whether the DTC is reproduced.




SST
09991-70201  

  1. Connect SST (TaSCAN) to DLC3.
  2. Turn IG ON.
  3. Following the SST (TaSCAN) display screen, clear the DTCs. (see)
  4. Turn the ignition OFF and wait for 30 seconds.
  5. Start the engine, turn all accessory switches OFF, and warm up the engine until the engine coolant temperature stabilizes. (Engine coolant temperature 75°C or higher) (A)
  6. Drive the vehicle at 60 to 100 km/h for 10 minutes or more. (B)
  7. Move the shift position to 2 gear. (C)
  8. To drive the vehicle at a speed suitable for fuel cut, keep the accelerator pedal depressed for 10 seconds or more and reach a speed of 60 km/h or more. (D)
  9. After depressing the accelerator pedal for 10 seconds or more, release the accelerator pedal for 4 seconds or more to perform fuel cut. (E)

    □ HINT □
    Fuel cut is performed when the accelerator pedal is released with the engine speed at approximately 1500 r/min or more. (Fuel cut stops when the engine speed is approximately 1000 r/min or less.)

  10. Decelerate the vehicle to 10 km/h. (F)
  11. Repeat steps (C) to (F) 3 times or more.
  12. Following the SST (TaSCAN) screen display, check the DTC judgment result. (see)

    ■ WARNING ■
    When performing this driving test, be sure to obey speed limits and other traffic laws.

    □ HINT □
    If the DTC judgment result is "judging" or "cannot judge", drive the vehicle again at 75 to 100 km/h for 10 minutes or more, repeat steps (C) to (F) 3 times or more, and then check the DTC judgment result.

The malfunctioning area can be identified by performing the [Injection Volume] active test. This Active Test can decrease the fuel injection volume by -12.5% or increase it by 24.8%.

  2. Turn IG ON.
  3. Start the engine, turn all accessory switches OFF, and warm up the engine until the engine coolant temperature stabilizes. (Engine coolant temperature is 75°C or higher)

□ HINT □
  1. If the air fuel ratio sensor output voltage remains at approximately 3.3 V in any of the conditions, including the above condition (see the malfunction case in the illustration), the air fuel ratio sensor may have an open circuit. (This also occurs when the air fuel ratio sensor heater has an open circuit.)
  2. If the air fuel ratio sensor output voltage remains at approximately 3.8 V or more, or 2.8 V or less in any of the conditions, the air fuel ratio sensor may have a short circuit.
  3. The engine control computer stops fuel injection during engine deceleration (fuel cut). This causes a lean condition and results in a momentary increase in the air fuel ratio sensor output voltage.
  4. While driving the vehicle:
    The air fuel ratio sensor output voltage may fall below 2.8 V during fuel enrichment. For the vehicle, this corresponds to sudden acceleration with the accelerator pedal fully depressed, such as when overtaking. The air fuel ratio sensor is functioning normally.
  5. The air fuel ratio sensor is a current output element. Therefore, the current is converted into voltage inside the engine control computer. Measuring the voltage at the air fuel ratio sensor connector or engine control computer connector will show a constant voltage result.
