DTC P0171/25 Lean Malfunction (Bank1)

Print

DTC P0172/26 Rich Malfunction (Bank1)

Preparation List


Circuit Description


  1. Detected when the fuel correction amount by the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) exceeds a specified value. There are 2 types of air-fuel ratio correction: normal operation air-fuel ratio correction (hereafter air-fuel ratio F/B) and operating condition memory air-fuel ratio correction (hereafter air-fuel ratio F/B learning). Air-fuel ratio F/B is the air-fuel ratio correction used to maintain the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio; the engine control computer receives the signal from the air fuel ratio sensor indicating whether the current air-fuel ratio is richer (hereafter rich) or leaner (hereafter lean) than the stoichiometric ratio, and decreases the injection volume when rich and increases it when lean. Air-fuel ratio F/B learning means that, when air-fuel ratio F/B is performed intermittently over a long period, the F/B learning target value changes to match individual engine differences (caused by aging, changes in operating environment, etc.). When both the air-fuel ratio F/B value and the air-fuel ratio F/B learning value exceed a specified value toward lean or rich, the engine control computer illuminates the check engine warning light.

Step 14Read TaSCAN Data
SST
09991-70200  

  1. Start the engine and maintain 2500r/min for 90 seconds to warm up the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor).

  1. Using SST (TaSCAN), check that the output voltage of the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) is normal.

  1. Shift into 1 gear.

  1. Accelerate to a vehicle speed of 60km/h, and check that the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage rises when the accelerator pedal is released (when deceleration fuel cut is performed).

    1. If the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage is fixed at 3.3V, the circuit may be open.
    2. If the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage is 3.8V or more or 2.8V or less, the circuit may be shorted.
    3. When the engine control computer performs deceleration fuel cut, the air-fuel ratio becomes lean and the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) voltage rises.
    4. The engine control computer learns the fully closed throttle position for fuel cut. If the battery terminal is disconnected, the learned value is cancelled, so it is necessary to drive at 16km/h or more to allow it to be learned.
    5. During acceleration, etc., the engine control computer increases the fuel volume, so the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) output voltage may be 2.8V or less.

    6. As the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) uses a current detection element, there is no voltage change even if the terminal voltage of the engine control computer is measured. The voltage displayed on SST (TaSCAN) is the current output by the air fuel ratio sensor (A/F sensor) converted to voltage.
