Continuously Variable Transmission Mechanism (CVT Mechanism)

●
Whereas a conventional automatic transaxle performs stepped shifting using planetary gears, the Super CVT-i automatic transaxle shifts using 1 pair of pulleys, whose groove width can be freely varied by hydraulic pressure, and a metal belt. During shifting, the pulley width is varied to shift steplessly. Furthermore, a wide gear ratio range ensures excellent fuel economy and quietness.

Continuously Variable Transmission Mechanism Components


•
It consists of pulleys with pistons and a metal belt, and shifting is performed by varying the pulley width through operation of the pistons in the primary and secondary pulleys.


CVT Pulley


•
A double piston construction has been adopted for the primary pulley, pressing the belt with the pressure generated in two hydraulic chambers in series. This allows the outer diameter of the hydraulic chambers to be reduced, making the system more compact.


CVT Metal Belt


•
The metal belt consists of metal elements and 2 rows of steel rings.
•
Whereas ordinary chains and rubber belts transmit power by tension, the metal belt transmits power by the compression of the elements (pushing of the elements).


Forward/Reverse Switching Mechanism


•
A single pinion type planetary gear has been adopted as the forward/reverse switching mechanism.
•
It consists of a reverse brake that holds the planetary carrier and a forward clutch that transmits engine driving force to the planetary sun gear.
•
A centrifugal hydraulic pressure cancel chamber is provided in the forward clutch to eliminate the centrifugal hydraulic pressure of the fluid generated by rotation, improving hydraulic response.

•
When moving forward, the forward clutch is engaged, and the driving force from the engine is input from the input shaft to the sun gear and output directly to the continuously variable transmission mechanism.

•
When reversing, the driving force from the engine is input to the ring gear. It is then input to the sun gear through the pinion gears, whose revolution is stopped by the reverse brake, and the driving force from the engine is output to the continuously variable transmission mechanism in the reverse direction of rotation.


Low Fuel Consumption


•
Compared with a conventional stepped A/T, driving close to the optimum fuel economy line (a preset high-torque range with good fuel consumption rate) is possible, drawing out the maximum engine performance. In addition, by expanding the lock-up range to the low speed side, the high-efficiency range can be used during acceleration and the fuel cut range can be expanded during deceleration, contributing to low fuel consumption. The wide gear ratio range lowers engine speed during high-speed driving by making use of the high gear, achieving excellent fuel economy.


CVT Power Performance


•
A characteristic of the CVT is that it can maintain the engine speed in the high-output range.
•
Unlike a conventional stepped A/T, a stepless CVT can accelerate while maintaining an efficient engine speed, so the maximum output range can be used efficiently and the driving force of the engine can be fully utilized.

•
In a conventional stepped A/T, the driving force characteristics of the engine (relationship between engine speed, torque and vehicle speed, etc.) are determined by each gear. Therefore, while the driving force characteristics lie on the curves drawn by each gear, with a CVT the driving force characteristics can be set freely. As a result, compared with the stepped driving force characteristics that occur with stepped shifting, smooth shift characteristics are achieved and engine characteristics can be fully utilized.
•
During vehicle acceleration, engine performance is fully utilized by shortening the initial time lag that occurs when the accelerator is depressed and by optimally controlling the driving force (engine speed x output shaft torque).
