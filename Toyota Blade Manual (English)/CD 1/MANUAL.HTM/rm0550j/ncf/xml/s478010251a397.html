Cranking Hold Control

●
When the start signal (STSW) from the engine switch is input, the engine control computer sends a signal (STAR) to the starter relay through the neutral start switch to operate the starter. At this time, the engine control computer turns ON the ACC cut (ACCR) to prevent flickering of the meter, clock, audio, etc.
●
After the starter starts operating, the engine control computer continues to output a signal to the starter until it detects that the engine has started running. After cranking, when the computer determines that the engine has started, it stops outputting the signal to the starter relay.

Related Parts


Crankshaft Position Sensor (Ne Signal)

●
A highly accurate electromagnetic pickup type sensor has been adopted. Detects the crank position and crank angular velocity.
●
The timing rotor on the crankshaft has 34 signal teeth with 2 teeth missing for top dead center detection. The crank position sensor detects the crank rotation signal every 10° and can accurately detect top dead center from the missing teeth area.
●
As the crankshaft rotates, the air gap between the timing rotor protrusions and the crank position sensor changes, so the magnetic flux passing through the coil of the crank position sensor increases and decreases, generating an electromotive force in the coil. Because this generated voltage is reversed when the timing rotor protrusion approaches and moves away from the cam position sensor, it appears as an AC voltage.

Water Temperature Sensor

Detects the engine coolant temperature and sends a signal to the engine control computer.
