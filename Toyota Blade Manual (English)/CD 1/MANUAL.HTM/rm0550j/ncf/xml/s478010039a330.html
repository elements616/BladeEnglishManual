Balance Shaft

●
Balance shafts have been adopted to cancel the 2-order inertia force that causes increased vibration and noise in large-displacement in-line 4-cylinder engines. Also, by optimizing the inertial mass of the balance shafts, low vibration and quietness (reduced vehicle booming noise) have been improved.
●
The drive method is direct drive by a gear press-fitted onto the #3 counterweight of the crankshaft.
●
Plastic gears have been adopted for driven gears No1 and No.3 to suppress gear noise. Also, friction loss has been reduced by making the bearings smaller in diameter, and the shaft length has been shortened, making the balance shafts among the lightest and most compact in the world.
●
A vertical wall has been formed in the balance shaft housing to reduce the flow of oil into the balance shaft area, reducing friction.

2-order Inertia Force


•
Cylinders No. 1 and No. 4, and cylinders No. 2 and No. 3, are in the same phase as each other and are 180° opposite in phase, so if the piston motion were perfect simple harmonic motion , the reciprocating inertia forces would cancel each other out. In an actual engine, due to the principle of the crank-piston mechanism, the piston speed is greatest on the top dead center side of the middle of the stroke even when the crankshaft rotation speed is constant, so higher-order (distortion) components are generated. These higher-order components occur 2 times per 1 crankshaft revolution, particularly in in-line 4-cylinder engines, and since they do not cancel but overlap each other, they cause increased vibration and noise. This component is the 2-order inertia force.


Effect of Balance Shafts


•
2 balance shafts are rotated at 2 times the speed of the crankshaft to cancel the 2-order inertia force. Also, by rotating the 2 shafts in opposite directions, the vibration generated by the balance shafts is canceled out.
