Lash Adjuster

●
Compact hydraulic lash adjusters have been adopted, constantly eliminating the gap (clearance) between the cams and rocker arms to improve engine quietness.
Construction of Lash Adjuster


•
The lash adjuster is located at the fulcrum on the opposite side of the rocker arm from the valve and uses oil supplied from the cylinder head as hydraulic fluid. The clearance is adjusted by the plunger in the body sliding up and down due to the reaction force of the plunger spring and hydraulic pressure.


Operation of Lash Adjuster


•
When the camshaft rotates clockwise and the cam pushes the rocker arm (during operation), a load is applied to both the valve and the plunger. At this time, the plunger tends to be pushed in, but the check ball closes, so the plunger stops. Therefore, the rocker arm tilts clockwise with the top of the plunger as a fulcrum, pushing the valve down.
•
When the cam passes its peak, the rocker arm begins to rise due to the force of the valve spring, but because the spring force of the valve spring also acts on the lash adjuster, the hydraulic pressure in the high-pressure chamber is maintained.
•
When the valve closes (end of operation), the oil in the high-pressure chamber is released from its pressurized state. At this time, valve clearance tends to occur, but the spring force of the plunger spring causes the plunger to push up the rocker arm, keeping the valve clearance at zero at all times. At the same time, the volume of the high-pressure chamber increases, creating a pressure difference with the low-pressure chamber; when it exceeds the spring force of the check ball spring, the check ball lowers to open the oil passage, and oil flows from the low-pressure chamber into the high-pressure chamber in preparation for the next operation (standby state).
