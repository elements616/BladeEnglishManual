Torque Sensor

●
A Hall IC type torque sensor is used.
●
The torque sensor is built into the steering column and converts the torque input during steering into an electrical signal. A multi-pole magnet is located on the input shaft (steering wheel side) and a yoke is located on the output shaft (steering gear side), and the input shaft and output shaft are connected via a torsion bar.
●
Coaxially outside the yoke are a flux collecting ring and Hall ICs, which are resin-molded and integrated with the circuit board. When steering torque is applied to the steering wheel, the torsion bar twists, causing relative displacement between the multi-pole magnet and the yoke; the resulting change in magnetic flux density is detected by the Hall ICs and output as a torque signal to the electric power steering computer.
●
The sensing section is a 2-fold redundant system consisting of 2 mutually independent Hall ICs, and diagnosis is performed by monitoring the torque signal values of Hall ICs 1,2.
●
The use of the Hall IC type made it possible to shorten the axial length of the torque sensor, ensuring sufficient steering column contraction for the collision safety mechanism.
