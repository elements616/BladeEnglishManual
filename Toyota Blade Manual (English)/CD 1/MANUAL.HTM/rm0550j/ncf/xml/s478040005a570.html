Electronically Controlled Coupling

●
The electronically controlled coupling consists of a main clutch, control clutch, front housing, shaft, armature, cam, piston and electromagnetic solenoid.
●
2 types of clutches are provided: the control clutch and the main clutch. The aim is to amplify the torque generated by the control clutch with the cam and generate even greater torque with the main clutch, producing sufficient torque while keeping the actuator (electromagnetic solenoid + control clutch + armature) compact and achieving a high level of torque generation response.
●
The control clutch is a metal multi-plate clutch so that it can pass magnetic flux, and because the main clutch generates several times the torque of the control clutch and does a large amount of work, a wet multi-plate clutch has been adopted for it. The surface treatment and groove shape of the metal clutch have been refined to ensure high durability and driving force transmission efficiency.
●
For both the main clutch and control clutch, the outer side is engaged with the front housing and the inner side with the shaft (the control clutch is engaged with the cam).

Operation of Electronically Controlled Coupling




When Not Operating
•
Driving force from the transfer is transmitted from the propeller shaft to the front housing. When the electromagnetic solenoid is not operating, the main clutch and control clutch are each free, so driving force is not transmitted from the front housing to the shaft, and driving force from the transfer is not transmitted to the rear wheels.



When Operating
•
When current flows through the electromagnetic solenoid, magnetic flux is generated around the electromagnetic solenoid. (A non-magnetic section is provided in the rear housing so that the magnetic flux does not converge only at the electromagnetic solenoid.) The attraction force of this electromagnet pulls the armature toward the control clutch, engaging the control clutch.
•
When the control clutch is engaged while there is a rotation difference between the front and rear wheels, the balls push on the slope of the cam engaged with the shaft, and the cam rotates. The rotation of the cam causes the piston to press against the main clutch, engaging the main clutch.
•
Driving force from the front housing is transmitted through the main clutch to the shaft and then to the rear differential, transmitting driving force to the rear wheels.



Operation Control
•
When there is a rotation difference between the front and rear wheels, the amount of driving force transmitted to the rear wheels is steplessly controlled by controlling the current flowing through the electromagnetic solenoid.
•
When the current is small, the force with which the armature restrains the control clutch is small, so the force with which the cam presses the piston is weak and the pressure applied to the main clutch is low. As a result, the driving force transmitted by the main clutch is small, and the driving force from the propeller shaft is transmitted to the rear wheels in a limited amount.
•
When the current increases, the force with which the armature restrains the control clutch becomes stronger, the force with which the cam presses the piston also becomes stronger, and the pressure applied to the main clutch increases. As a result, the driving force transmitted by the main clutch increases, and power is transmitted in a state close to direct-coupled 4WD.
