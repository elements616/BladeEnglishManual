Active Torque Control 4WD Outline

●
Active Torque Control 4WD has been adopted. By controlling the transmitted torque of the electronically controlled coupling located at the front of the rear differential, the front/rear torque distribution is continuously varied between the front-wheel drive state and direct-coupled 4WD, achieving optimum driving force distribution under various driving conditions for stability and low fuel consumption.
●
A differential limiting mechanism, as in a typical full-time 4WD, is not required, and the system has been simplified to reduce size and weight.


Features of Active Torque Control 4WD

Traction Performance
Stable starting and acceleration on slippery roads such as snowy roads, equivalent to direct-coupled 4WD
Stability
Stable cornering performance
Fuel Economy
Low fuel consumption by distributing torque to the rear wheels only when needed and only as much as needed

●
The torque transmitted to the rear wheels is controlled by the Active Torque Control 4WD computer to achieve the optimum front/rear torque distribution based on input signals from various sensors.

Main Components and Functions


Main Components
Function
4WD Auto Mode Switch
Switches between 4WD auto mode and FF (front-wheel drive) mode.
4WD Auto Mode Indicator
Informs the driver of the 4WD auto mode control status by illuminating the light. When 4WD auto mode is ON, the light blinks 2 times and then illuminates, and when 4WD auto mode is OFF, the light goes off. In addition, if excessive driving during 4WD control may damage the drive system, the light blinks.
Master Warning Indicator Light
Warns the driver of a 4WD system malfunction by illuminating the light.
Multi-information Display
Informs the driver of a 4WD system malfunction by illuminating the master warning indicator light and displaying it on the multi-information display.
Semiconductor Type Wheel Speed Sensor & Rotor
Detects the wheel speed of each of the 4 wheels and inputs it to the skid control computer.
Stop Light Switch
Detects the brake operation state and inputs it to the skid control computer.
Parking Brake Switch
Detects parking brake operation and inputs it to the main body ECU (instrument panel junction block assembly).
Shift Position Sensor
Detects the shift position and inputs it to the engine control computer.
Throttle Position Sensor
Inputs the throttle valve opening to the engine control computer.
Crankshaft Position Sensor
Detects the engine speed and inputs it to the engine control computer.
Electronically Controlled Coupling
Distributes transmitted torque to the rear wheels according to the output signal from the Active Torque Control 4WD computer.
Skid Control Computer
Detects the signals from the semiconductor type wheel speed sensors, G sensor and stop light switch and outputs them to the Active Torque Control 4WD computer via CAN communication.
Engine Control Computer
Detects the signals from the shift position sensor, throttle position sensor and crank position sensor and outputs them to the Active Torque Control 4WD computer via CAN communication.
Main Body ECU (Instrument Panel Junction Block Assembly)
Detects the parking brake switch signal and outputs it to the Active Torque Control 4WD computer via CAN communication.
Active Torque Control 4WD Computer
Detects the driving condition from the signals from each ECU, sensor and switch, and outputs a control signal to the electronically controlled coupling to distribute the optimum torque to the rear wheels according to the driving situation.

4WD Auto Mode


•
Turning the 4WD auto mode switch ON switches to 4WD auto mode, and control is performed to achieve both "traction performance" and "avoidance of the tight corner braking phenomenon".
Normal Control


•
During normal driving, the engine driving torque is calculated from signals such as throttle opening, engine speed and shift position, and the torque transmitted to the rear wheels is optimally controlled to ensure maximum acceleration performance.
•
When it is determined from the throttle opening signal and wheel speed signals that the driver's operation and the vehicle are stable, the torque transmitted to the rear wheels is lowered to a state close to front-wheel drive, contributing to improved fuel economy.



Start-off Control


By optimally distributing the total driving torque estimated from the engine torque to the front and rear wheels, starting performance equivalent to direct-coupled 4WD is ensured. In addition, to avoid the tight corner braking phenomenon*that occurs during low-speed turning, etc., torque transmission to the rear wheels is optimized.



HINT
*
The tight corner braking phenomenon is a condition in which, when a rotation difference between the front and rear wheels is required, such as during turning, a 4WD vehicle with directly coupled front and rear wheels cannot absorb this rotation difference when driving in 4-wheel drive, resulting in a state as if the brakes were applied.
Deceleration Control


During braking deceleration, 4WD auto mode is canceled to increase the effectiveness of ABS and VSC control.



FF Mode


•
When the 4WD auto mode switch is turned OFF, 4WD control is stopped and torque transmission to the rear wheels is cut, resulting in an FF (front-wheel drive) state. This enables driving aimed at improving fuel economy.

Fail-safe Function


•
If there is a malfunction in the 4WD system, the master warning indicator light illuminates, "4WD Check" is displayed on the multi-information display, and 4WD control is stopped and the vehicle switches to front-wheel drive.
•
If excessive driving may damage the drive system, the 4WD auto mode indicator blinks, and 4WD control is stopped and the vehicle switches to front-wheel drive. By cooling the drive system without turning off the engine, 4WD control becomes possible again.
