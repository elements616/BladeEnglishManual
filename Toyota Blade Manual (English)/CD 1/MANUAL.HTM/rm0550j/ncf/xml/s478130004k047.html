Outer Rear View Mirror

●
"Retractable remote control outer mirrors with turn signal lights" are standard on all models. For details, refer to the Power Mirror System.
●
A rain clearing blue mirror, which combines a rain clearing mirror layer with functions such as a self-cleaning effect and a blue mirror layer with low reflectance in the daytime and high reflectance at night, has been adopted on the mirror surface to ensure stable rear visibility.
●
Cold area specification models have a heater inside the mirror.

Rain Clearing Blue Mirror


•
The rain clearing blue mirror is a front-surface mirror with a Cr reflective layer coated on the glass surface, and its structure prevents double images through surface reflection.
•
Through the interference effect of the 2 layers, with a titania layer coated on the surface of the Cr reflective layer, and a reflectance adjustment layer sandwiched in between, the reflected light is made blue without tinting the glass. This enhances the effect of reducing glare from reflected light and the effect of cutting ultraviolet and infrared rays.
•
The silica layer has a hydrophilic effect that spreads water droplets adhering to the surface into a thin film. In addition, the hydrophilic effect is enhanced by giving the silica layer surface an uneven texture.
•
The titania layer has a self-cleaning effect that chemically decomposes and removes dirt adhering to the silica layer surface through photocatalytic action that reacts with sunlight (ultraviolet rays).


Hydrophilic Effect


•
Material surfaces in the atmosphere are broadly divided into hydrophilic surfaces, which bond easily with water, and hydrophobic surfaces, which do not bond easily (tend to repel water). Ordinary mirror surfaces are covered with hydrophobic substances (dirt, etc.), so when water adheres, it forms hemispherical droplets due to surface tension.
•
The rain clearing blue mirror has a hydrophilic silica layer on its surface that bonds easily with water, and the outermost surface of the silica has an uneven structure to hold a large amount of water. As a result, when water adheres, it is spread into a thin film to ensure visibility. If dirt adheres directly to the outermost surface of the silica layer, the hydrophilic effect may decrease, but when exposed to sunlight (ultraviolet rays), the hydrophilic effect can be restored by the self-cleaning effect.


Self-cleaning Effect (Photocatalytic Action)


•
When exposed to sunlight (ultraviolet rays), electrons (e-) are generated inside the titania layer, and the electrons pass through the silica layer and are adsorbed by and excite the surrounding oxygen (O2), changing it into active oxygen (O2-). (Fig. A) This active oxygen chemically reacts with dirt (organic matter) adhering to the silica layer surface, decomposing the dirt into carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O) and removing it. Variable Reflection Effect


•
The reflected color is blue, and by lowering the reflectance of yellow and red light (long wavelength range), which feels particularly glaring in the bright "daytime", glare from sunlight and headlights is reduced.
•
In the dark "nighttime", reflectance equivalent to that of an untinted mirror is ensured to maintain visibility. In addition, the effect of cutting infrared and ultraviolet rays is also enhanced.


Handling Precautions


•
Washing with water alone is sufficient when cleaning the mirror surface. Rubbing the mirror surface with an oil film remover (compound, etc.) or a cloth with sand on it, or applying wax, water repellent, etc., may damage or attack the silica/titania layers coated on the mirror surface and cause loss of the hydrophilic effect.
•
If the mirror surface is frozen, do not scrape it off with a plastic plate, etc.; thaw it with running water or warm water.


Temporary Decrease in Hydrophilic Effect
•
Under the following conditions, the hydrophilic effect temporarily decreases, but this is not a malfunction. The hydrophilic effect recovers with several days of sunlight; to restore the effect quickly, clean the mirror according to the procedure described in the Repair Manual.

Conditions Causing Temporary Decrease in Hydrophilic Effect

•
When the mirror surface is touched with bare hands, or dirt on the mirror surface is wiped off with an oily cloth, etc.
•
When the vehicle is parked for a long period in a place not exposed to sunlight, such as an underground parking lot.
•
When wax remover used to remove vehicle wax adheres.
•
When wax adheres from an automatic car wash.
