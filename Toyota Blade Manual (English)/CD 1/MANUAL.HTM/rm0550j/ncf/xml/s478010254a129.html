Cylinder Block

●
Centrifugally cast iron liners cast into the block have been adopted for the cylinder liners. A spiny treatment is applied to the outer surface of the cylinder liners (cast iron) to improve adhesion with the cylinder bores (aluminum) and heat dissipation, reducing piston temperature and suppressing bore deformation.
●
Rigidity has been increased by curving the outer walls of the block and providing reinforcing ribs on the transmission mounting side.
●
The arrangement of the oil return holes from the cylinder head and the blow-by gas passages has been optimized to smooth oil return and increase the rigidity of the sides of the cylinder block.
●
Coolant passages formed by drilled passages are provided between the bores, actively cooling the upper part of the cylinder bores, which is easily affected by heat, and making the circumferential temperature distribution of the bore walls uniform. This improves knock resistance and minimizes thermal deformation between the cylinder bores, reducing friction and achieving high performance and low fuel consumption.
●
Knock sensors are located at 1 locations each on the left and right inside the V-bank, enabling optimum knock control.
●
Cast iron crankshaft bearing caps have been adopted. They are tightened using plastic region tightening and secured from below with 4 bolts. In addition, they are also tightened together with the cylinder block from both sides to increase rigidity and reduce vibration and noise.
