WIL Concept Seat


•
In a low-speed rear-end collision, the WIL concept seat maintains the angle between the occupant's head and torso and supports them firmly, minimizing the relative movement of the head and torso and reducing the impact on the neck.

•
The upper part of the seatback frame is shaped away from the occupant and a thick seatback pad has been adopted, creating a structure that allows the occupant's torso to sink in and brings the head closer to the headrest in a low-speed rear-end collision.
•
The seatback springs support the occupant over a wide area from the back to the pelvis and can absorb the impact.
•
The headrest is positioned close to the occupant's head and at a high position so that it can firmly support the head.
•
In addition to the conventional WIL concept seat, the following 3 elements have been incorporated to reduce whiplash.

Manual Lifter Mechanism


•
The entire seat is raised and lowered by operating the pump type lifter knob on the outer side of the driver seat cushion.
•
The lifter mechanism consists of the pump type lifter knob, lifter gear, lifter rod, connecting link, bell crank, etc.

•
To raise the seat, operate the pump type lifter knob in the direction of arrow C; the pinion gear rotates in the same direction in conjunction. As a result, the lifter gear rotates in the direction of arrow E around fulcrum Q.
•
A connecting link is attached to the lifter gear; when the lifter gear rotates in the direction of arrow E, the connecting link moves in direction F and the bell crank rotates in the direction of arrow G around fulcrum R. As the bell crank rotates in the direction of arrow G and lifts the rear end of the seat, the front lifter link rotates in the direction of arrow H around fulcrum S, and the entire seat lifts up. A brake mechanism is incorporated in the linkage between the pump type lifter knob and pinion gear, so the pinion gear does not reverse due to the load from the seat cushion.
•
To lower the seat, operate the pump type lifter knob in the direction opposite to arrow C. The lifter adjustment range is approximately 46mm.


Front Power Seat

Front Power Seat

●
The power seat adopts an adjustment mechanism with electric adjusters, allowing easy position adjustment. In addition, the power seat switch is located on the outer shield on the right side of the driver seat.

Power Slide Mechanism


•
When the slide adjustment of the power seat switch is operated, the slide motor rotates. This rotation is transmitted to the seat slide screw via the worm gear in the seat slide housing.
•
The seat slide screw meshes with a resin nut; because the resin nut is fixed to the seat track lower rail via the nut hold bracket, the transmitted rotation moves the seat slide screw forward and rearward relative to the seat track lower rail. As a result, the seat track upper rail to which the seat slide screw is attached slides. The slide can be adjusted approximately 240 mm forward and rearward.


Power Lifter Mechanism


•
When the lifter adjustment of the power seat switch is operated, the lifter motor rotates. This rotation moves the lifter screw forward and rearward via the worm gear in the lifter housing.
•
A connecting link is attached to the tip of the lifter screw and transmits the forward and rearward movement of the lifter screw to the bell crank.
•
The bell crank transmits the movement of the lifter screw to the outer side via the lifter rod and rotates around fulcrum R, lifting the rear end of the lower arm. In conjunction with this, the front lifter link rotates around fulcrum S, lifting the front end of the lower arm, and the entire seat lifts up.
•
The lifter can be adjusted within a range of approximately 50mm up and down.


Power Reclining Mechanism


•
The reclining mechanism consists of an external gear on the seatback frame side, an internal gear on the lower arm side, a reclining hinge pin that regulates the meshing of the external and internal gears, a reclining motor and reclining gear box that rotate the reclining hinge pin, and a reclining rod that transmits the seatback movement to the inner side.
•
The reclining adjustment angle is 8° forward and 42° rearward from the neutral position.

•
When the reclining motor is driven by operating the reclining switch, this rotation is transmitted to the reclining hinge pin via the reclining gear box.
•
The reclining hinge pin has a cam section with center point B offset from the hinge pin rotation axis point A; when the hinge pin rotates, the cam section rotates along a loop-shaped path (Fig. A).
•
An external gear is provided on the cam section of the reclining hinge pin and meshes with the internal gear on the seat cushion (lower arm) side. The internal gear has 1 more teeth than the external gear, so each time the internal gear makes 1 revolution along the loop-shaped path (Fig. A), the meshing position of the external and internal gears changes and the seatback frame gradually moves.


Power Front Vertical Mechanism


•
When the front vertical adjustment of the power seat switch is operated, the front vertical motor rotates. This rotation moves the front vertical screw forward and rearward via the worm gear in the front vertical housing.
•
A link is attached to the tip of the front vertical screw; the forward and rearward movement of the front vertical screw rotates the link, raising and lowering the front end of the seat cushion. The link also transmits the movement to the link on the inner side via the front vertical rod.
•
The front vertical can be adjusted 5° up and down.

Seat Heater Operation


•
When the seat heater switch is turned ON in HI mode, current flows from the thermostat to all seat heaters, warming the seat. When the seat heater switch is turned ON in LO mode, current flows from the thermostat to a specific portion of the seatback heater, warming the seatback. The thermostat turns OFF when the seat warms up, cutting off current to the seat heater, and turns ON when the seat temperature drops, resuming current to the seat heater. This operation keeps the seat surface temperature at approximately 40°C in HI mode and approximately 36°C in LO mode.

Seat Lock Operation
•
Raise the folded seatback to engage the hook on the side of the seatback with the striker. At this time, the lock release button is slightly depressed, indicating as a warning margin (5.1mm) that the seat is not locked.
•
After raising the seatback and confirming that the striker contacts the hook, push the seatback firmly. Then, section A of the striker slides along the surface of the hook and fits into the recess of the hook. This is called the first-stage lock engaged state.

•
In the first-stage lock engaged state, pressing the seat lock release button down by the first-stage lock release stroke (16.6mm) pulls up the rod, rotating the hook around A to release it from the striker, allowing the seatback to be folded forward.

•
From the first-stage lock engaged state, pressing the seat lock release button down by the second-stage lock release stroke (23.8mm) pulls up the rod, rotating the hook around A and releasing it from the striker. Then push the seatback, and when it stops, release the seat lock release button to engage the hook with the striker. This is called the second-stage lock engaged state.
•
In the second-stage lock engaged state, pressing the seat lock release button down by the second-stage lock release stroke (23.8mm) pulls up the rod, rotating the hook around A to release it from the striker, allowing the seatback to be folded forward.

ETC System Outline

ETC System Outline

●
An ETC on-board unit <toll collection (ETC) computer assembly and integrated antenna (navigation antenna assembly)> compatible with the ETC (Electronic Toll Collection) system has been adopted, and is offered as an option on all models as a set with the HDD navigation system.
●
The ETC system performs the exchanges required for toll collection through wireless communication between the "ETC on-board unit" and the "ETC roadside radio unit", eliminating the need to stop to pay at toll road toll gates; its purposes are "improved convenience through cashless payment", "relief of toll gate congestion by reducing the time required for toll collection" and "contributing to environmental improvement near toll gates through non-stop passage".
●
The ETC on-board unit consists of the ETC unit, the integrated antenna (navigation antenna assembly) and the ETC card; tolls are charged automatically when the vehicle passes through a toll road toll gate with the ETC card inserted in the ETC unit.
●
A display function that shows tolls and various settings on the multi-display screen of the HDD navigation has been adopted.

Features of the G-BOOK System




Real-time Information Acquisition
•
By accessing the G-BOOK Center via the customer's cellular phone connected by Bluetooth, new map data (Map On Demand), route searches that take congestion into account (G Route Search), news and various other information can be obtained.


Text-to-speech
•
In consideration of safety while driving, text data can be read aloud.* This allows news, e-mail, etc. to be checked through the vehicle speakers.
HINT
*
If there is an incoming call on the customer's cellular phone connected by Bluetooth, or ETC or navigation voice guidance, while G-BOOK content is being read aloud, the reading of the G-BOOK content is temporarily suspended and the telephone, ETC or navigation voice is played. When playback ends, the reading of the G-BOOK content resumes.

Obtaining Information (Contents) in the Vehicle


•
G-BOOK mX information (contents) is obtained by data communication from the G-BOOK network, centered on the G-BOOK Center, using the customer's Bluetooth compatible cellular phone.

G-BOOK Settings


•
To use G-BOOK, various settings matching the connected communication device (the customer's Bluetooth compatible cellular phone) are required.
•
Settings for using the G-BOOK service are made from the screen displayed by touching the "G-BOOK" TAB on the settings/edit screen. For details, refer to G-BOOK under settings/edit.


G-BOOK.com


•
G-BOOK.com is a user's own menu folder in which convenient preset contents can be viewed and executed, and which can be customized by adding G-BOOK's extensive contents to create the user's own G-BOOK.
•
The G-BOOK.com screen is displayed by touching "G-BOOK.com" on the main menu screen; touching the touch switch frame of each menu displayed there moves to that menu.


G Memory Point Information


•
Facilities, etc. saved on G-BOOK.com or GAZOO.com can be displayed in a list.
•
Registered G memory point information can also be recalled from the memory points in navigation mode and set as a destination.

Browsing History


•
The titles of contents viewed in the browser are saved in order from newest date, making it easy to search when you want to view previously used contents again.

Bookmark


•
By registering the page displayed in the browser as a bookmark, a page you want to view again can be easily displayed from the bookmark screen.
•
The titles of registered bookmarks can also be edited (changed) as desired.

About the Browser


•
The browser is an application that displays the various contents provided by G-BOOK.com.
•
The browser screens consist of a screen displayed while viewing contents and a screen displayed while contents are being read aloud.
•
Automatic reading by the browser can be set to "On"/"Off" from "G-BOOK Detailed Settings" on the settings/edit screen. When set to "On", the reading speed can be set to "Normal"/"Slow".

Emergency Notification Flow






Preparation for First Use
•

HELPNET Service Start Operation
•
A communication test with the HELPNET Center, confirmation of member registration with the HELPNET Center and inspection of the system equipment can be performed manually.*
HINT
*
The service start operation must always be performed by the contract holder.


Means of Notification
•
Notification is made by pressing the "INFO/G" switch on the panel switches and touching "HELPNET" on the displayed G-BOOK screen.
G-BOOK Screen






In the following cases, the emergency notification service may not be available or its quality may be reduced.
•
When the G-BOOK online service contract is not established or has expired, or when the service start operation has not been performed after application and registration.
•
When communication is not possible due to a malfunction of HELPNET compatible equipment or vehicle-related equipment.
•
When a center position determination error occurs.
•
When communication is not possible due to a malfunction of the center system.
•
When communication is not possible due to the communication network used by the emergency notification system.
•
When service is interrupted or suspended for reasons of the emergency notification provider.


Precautions
•
During emergency notification, HELPNET has priority, so the cellular phone cannot be used from the HDD navigation system.
•
If emergency notification starts while the cellular phone is in use, use of the cellular phone is stopped.
•
Depending on radio wave conditions, emergency notification and calls may not be possible.
•
Outside the cellular phone service area, emergency notification is not possible even if the emergency notification operation starts. If emergency notification is not possible, make the report from the nearest public telephone, etc.
•
In a vehicle collision, if equipment necessary for communication malfunctions or is damaged, emergency notification and calls may not be possible.
•
Emergency notification may not be possible if the battery voltage drops or the connection to the battery is cut.
•
While waiting for rescue after emergency notification, move to a safe place where conversation with the center is possible.
•
Perform emergency notification by button operation after stopping the vehicle in a safe place.
•
Do not use functions that restrict outgoing or incoming calls (dial lock, etc.) as they will interfere with emergency notification. The functions that interfere with emergency notification differ depending on the telephone or communication carrier used.

G-BOOK and Navigation Linked Functions




Function Outline
•
By linking the navigation with the G-BOOK online service, road conditions such as congestion are provided in real time. Main functions include G Route Search (with probe information) and congestion prediction information.


G Route Search (with Probe Information)
•
Comprehensively predicts future road conditions from the latest road traffic information by VICS information and probe communication traffic information and from past statistical data, and searches for and guides an optimal route that avoids congestion.


Congestion Prediction Information
•
Displays predicted congestion time, predicted congestion increase/decrease, etc. based on congestion prediction information from the G-BOOK Center.
Congestion Prediction Information


Service Name
Service Contents
Area Check
Predicts the latest road traffic information for expressways around the current location and road conditions up to 90 minutes ahead, displays future congestion on an easy-to-understand simplified map on the navigation screen and provides voice guidance.
Route Check
Predicts the latest road traffic information for expressways, main toll roads and some general roads on the route to the destination and road conditions up to 90 minutes ahead, displays it on an easy-to-understand simplified map on the navigation screen and provides voice guidance.
Time Required Check
Simulates, based on congestion prediction, the time required to the next expressway exit when taking a break (30 or 60 minutes) at an expressway service area, and clearly displays the difference in driving time for each break time.
