Anti-squat / Anti-lift Geometry


•
The instantaneous center (OR) of the suspension geometry has been brought closer to the height of the axle center. This suppresses squat during acceleration and lift during braking.


Anti-squat Geometry


•
When driving force is generated at the rear wheels during acceleration, etc., the load on the front and rear wheels changes due to inertia. As a result, the load on the rear wheels increases and the rear of the vehicle squats. In anti-squat geometry, the geometric intersection point (OR) of the suspension is brought closer to the axle center; when the reaction force (T) of the driving force acting on the axle center tries to move the vehicle forward via the intersection point (OR), because there is an angle between the reaction force of the driving force and the direction of the intersection point, component forces are generated in the direction of the intersection point (T1) and in the direction of the ground contact surface (T2). By reducing this component force in the direction of the ground contact surface, squat at the rear of the vehicle during acceleration is suppressed.


Anti-lift Geometry


•
Load transfer during braking reduces the load on the rear wheels, causing the rear of the vehicle to lift. In anti-lift geometry, the geometric intersection point (OR) of the suspension is set higher than the ground contact surface. As a result, when the braking force (BR) acting on the rear wheels is supported at the intersection point (OR), component forces are generated toward the intersection point (BR1) and acting on the ground contact surface (BR2). Because the force acting on the ground contact surface is in the opposite direction to the tire reaction force against the sprung load reduced by braking, lift at the rear of the vehicle can be suppressed.


Electric Power Steering Outline

Electric Power Steering Outline

●
Electric power steering has been adopted on all models.
●
Electric power steering generates assist torque during steering by means of a motor and reduction mechanism mounted on the column shaft, reducing the steering effort. Therefore, because assist force is generated only during steering, excellent fuel economy is achieved.
●
The direction and amount of assist force are determined by the electric power steering computer based on the signal from the torque sensor built into the steering column and the meter vehicle speed signal. Control is performed to reduce the steering effort at low speeds and to provide a firm steering feel at high speeds, ensuring an excellent steering feel.

Detects the twist of the torsion bar, converts the torque generated during steering into an electrical signal, and outputs it to the electric power steering computer.
Rotation Angle Sensor (Built into Motor)
Outputs the rotation angle of the motor to the electric power steering computer.

Combination Meter
When there is a system malfunction, the warning light in the meter is illuminated by a signal from the electric power steering computer.

Features


Item
Features, etc.
Low Fuel Consumption
Whereas hydraulic power steering constantly operates a hydraulic pump using engine power, electric power steering drives the motor only during steering when assist torque is required. As a result, no energy is consumed by the power steering during straight-ahead driving such as at high speeds, achieving excellent fuel economy.
Simplified Construction
A simple configuration has been achieved by building the electric power steering components (motor, reduction mechanism and torque sensor) into the steering column and locating the electric power steering computer at the top of the steering column.
Elimination of Hydraulic System
The hydraulic piping and driving fluid required for hydraulic power steering are no longer needed, making removal and installation of the steering gear easier.
Weight Reduction
Weight reduction has been achieved through the elimination of the piping system and the more compact system.
Steering Feel
By optimizing the assist characteristics based on the meter vehicle speed sensor signal, a steering feel that is light at low speeds and firm at high speeds is achieved.


Aerodynamic Performance

Aerodynamic Performance

●
Excellent aerodynamic performance has been achieved through flush surfaces on all parts of the upper body, engine under covers and other parts on the underbody that control the airflow under the floor, and a flat underfloor, ensuring a high level of handling stability and straight-line stability at high speeds.
●
A rear spoiler has been provided at the rear end of the roof panel, with a design matched to the flow from the roof panel, to straighten the airflow and reduce driving resistance.

●
The front fender liners have a tunnel shape that guides the airflow under the vehicle to the brake calipers & rotors, improving brake fade resistance.
●
A recessed shape has been provided on the surface of the fender liner close to the front side of the tire to straighten the airflow in front of the tire at high speeds, improving the steering response.
