ABS with EBD

ABS with EBD

●
For a vehicle to drive, turn and stop, it is fundamental that the tires grip the road surface and perform their function as tires. However, during sudden braking or braking on slippery road surfaces such as snowy roads or puddles, the wheels may lock while the vehicle is still moving, causing slipping between the tires and the road surface. Therefore, ABS (Anti-lock Brake System) detects the wheel speed during such braking and controls the brake fluid pressure of all 4 wheels under computer control. This control prevents the tires from locking and ensures sufficient braking performance, thus maintaining steerability and vehicle stability.
●
In addition to conventional ABS functions, ABS with EBD appropriately controls the braking force of the front and rear wheels and of the left and right wheels regardless of load changes due to the vehicle being empty or loaded, ensuring excellent braking performance.

Front/Rear Brake Force Distribution Control


•
Front/rear brake force distribution control achieves an appropriate distribution of braking force to the front and rear wheels according to the vehicle driving conditions, with the aim of fully bringing out the "stopping" performance that is the basic function of the brakes.
•
As a result, the braking force of the rear wheels can be effectively used according to load changes due to loading conditions and deceleration, reducing the brake pedal effort particularly when loaded and ensuring excellent braking effectiveness.


Left/Right Brake Force Distribution Control


•
Left/right brake force distribution control controls the braking force of the left and right wheels to ensure vehicle stability when braking during cornering, ensuring excellent braking effectiveness while maintaining vehicle stability during braking.


Precautions for ABS with EBD


•
ABS is not a device that provides performance exceeding the capability of the tires; drive safely, paying sufficient attention to speed, following distance, etc.
•
Installing parts other than those specified, such as tires of other than the specified size, will adversely affect ABS control.
•
Motor noise may be heard due to the initial check immediately after the engine is started and the vehicle is driven for the first time; this is not a malfunction.
•
If the brake pedal is lightly depressed during the initial check, it may feel heavy; this is not a malfunction.
•
Under the following conditions, the braking distance when ABS operates may be longer than that of a vehicle without ABS.

Condition

When driving on gravel roads or fresh snow
When tire chains are installed
When driving over bumps such as road joints
When driving on rough roads such as uneven roads or cobblestones


TRC

TRC

●
If the throttle is opened too much when starting off or accelerating on a slippery road surface, etc., the drive wheels may slip due to excessive torque, impairing starting/acceleration performance and steerability. TRC is a system that suppresses drive wheel slip through brake fluid pressure control of the drive wheels and engine output control, controls the driving force according to road surface conditions, and ensures the vehicle's starting/acceleration performance, straight-line stability and cornering stability.

TRC Features

①
Delicate accelerator operation is not required for starting off and accelerating on slippery road surfaces
②
Excellent steerability and stability during acceleration
③
More stable cornering even when accelerating during a turn
④
Stable acceleration even on split road surfaces where the conditions under the left and right drive wheels differ


VSC

VSC

●
Whereas ABS and TRC are mainly intended to ensure stability during braking and acceleration associated with brake and accelerator operation, VSC is a system that ensures the stability of the vehicle in the turning direction. Normally, the vehicle turns stably in accordance with steering operation, but depending on road surface conditions, vehicle speed, unexpected situations such as emergency turns, or external factors, it may tend toward strong oversteer or strong understeer. In such cases, VSC automatically controls engine output and the brakes of each wheel to ensure stability, mitigating strong understeer or strong oversteer.
Control Performance Outline


•
VSC detects the vehicle condition from the signals from various sensors such as the yaw rate sensor & linear G sensor, and controls brake fluid pressure and engine output torque.
•
The following are examples of situations in which the tires exceed their lateral grip limit.

Examples of Situations Exceeding the Grip Limit

①
When the rear wheels are losing grip relative to the front wheels (strong oversteer condition)
②
When the front wheels are losing grip relative to the rear wheels (strong understeer condition)

•
VSC operates in situations such as those shown in the illustration below to mitigate the vehicle's tendency toward strong oversteer or understeer.


Determination of Strong Oversteer Tendency


•
The oversteer tendency is determined by the skid control computer from the vehicle slip angle and slip angular velocity obtained from the yaw rate sensor & linear G sensor. That is, when the vehicle slip angle is large and the slip angular velocity is also large, the vehicle can be said to have an oversteer tendency.


Determination of Strong Understeer Tendency


•
The understeer tendency is determined by the skid control computer from the target yaw rate and the actual yaw rate of the vehicle. That is, when the driver steers and the actual vehicle yaw rate is smaller than the target yaw rate that should be generated (determined from the steering amount and vehicle speed), this means that the vehicle is not turning, so the understeer tendency can be said to be large.


VSC Operation Method




Mitigation of Strong Oversteer Tendency
•
When the skid control computer determines that the oversteer tendency is large, it applies the brake to the front wheel on the outside of the turn according to the degree of the tendency, generating an outward moment on the vehicle to suppress the oversteer tendency. In addition, the reduction in vehicle speed due to the braking force contributes to ensuring vehicle stability.


Mitigation of Strong Understeer Tendency
•
When the skid control computer determines that the understeer tendency is large, it controls engine output according to the degree of the tendency and applies the brakes to the front and rear wheels to reduce lateral force, suppressing the understeer tendency.


S-VSC(Steering-assisted Vehicle Stability Control)

S-VSC(Steering-assisted Vehicle Stability Control)

●
Cooperative control between VSC and the electric power steering system is performed. By integrating these preventive safety functions, excellent driving stability and handling have been ensured.
●
Steering cooperative control is performed during braking/driving on split road surfaces and during oversteer or understeer tendencies.
●
When the vehicle slips and becomes unstable, this function controls the brake fluid pressure of the 4 wheels. At this time, EPS performs steering torque assist control to assist the driver's steering operation.

Control During Acceleration on Split Road Surfaces


•
If the throttle is opened too much when starting off or accelerating on a slippery road surface, etc., the drive wheels may slip due to excessive torque, impairing starting/acceleration performance and handling. Particularly on split road surfaces where the conditions under the left and right drive wheels differ, the vehicle may be pulled toward the side with the lower friction coefficient due to the difference in driving force between left and right. In addition to the conventional TRC function, S-VSC applies steering torque in the direction that cancels out the difference in left and right driving force, assisting the steering effort so that the driver can easily correct the vehicle's course.


Control During Braking on Split Road Surfaces


•
When braking suddenly on a road surface where the slipperiness differs between left and right, such as with the tires on one side on asphalt and the other on snow, the vehicle may be pulled toward the side with the higher friction coefficient due to the difference in braking force between left and right. In addition to the conventional ABS function, S-VSC applies steering torque in the direction that cancels out the difference in left and right braking force, assisting the steering effort so that the driver can easily correct the vehicle's course.


Control During Oversteer Tendency


•
The oversteer tendency is determined from the vehicle slip angle and slip angular velocity, and according to the degree of the tendency, the brakes are applied mainly to the wheels on the outside of the turn to generate an outward moment, suppressing the oversteer tendency. At the same time, an assist torque command in the oversteer correction direction is sent to the EPS, controlling the steering torque so that the driver can steer more easily.


Control During Understeer Tendency


•
The driver's requested yaw rate is calculated from the steering amount and vehicle speed and compared with the actual vehicle yaw rate to determine the understeer tendency. According to the degree of the tendency, engine output is suppressed and the brakes are applied, generating a moment in the same direction as the vehicle's turning direction and suppressing excessive understeer. At the same time, an EPS assist torque command is sent to prevent the driver from oversteering the steering wheel and worsening the understeer tendency.


Brake Assist System

Brake Assist System

●
Emergency braking is determined from the speed and amount of brake pedal depression, and a strong braking force is generated accordingly.
Brake Assist Concept


•
During emergency braking, a panicked driver may operate the brake quickly but be unable to depress it firmly, producing only a small braking force. (a in the illustration)
•
Also, such drivers may be unable to keep depressing the brake pedal for long in the latter half of braking, causing the braking force to decrease. (b in the illustration)
•
When the brake is depressed quickly, "Brake Assist" infers the driver's intention to brake in an emergency and ensures braking force even if the driver cannot depress the pedal very firmly. (c in the illustration)
•
After it operates, when the driver intentionally releases the brake, "Brake Assist" reduces the amount of braking force assist to reduce any feeling of discomfort. (d in the illustration)


Precautions for Brake Assist


•
Brake assist is not a device that provides performance exceeding the actual capability of the brakes. Drive safely, paying sufficient attention to vehicle speed, following distance, etc.

Hill-start Assist Control

Hill-start Assist Control

●
Hill-start assist control holds and controls the brake fluid pressure of the 4 wheels to prevent the vehicle from rolling backward when the driver moves from the brake pedal to the accelerator pedal when starting off on a slope.



Operating Conditions
●
Hill-start assist control operation starts when the brake pedal is depressed with all of the following conditions met.
●
When operation starts, the buzzer sounds, the slip indicator in the combination meter blinks and the stop lights illuminate.
Operating Conditions


1
When a shift position other than "P" is selected
2
Vehicle is stopped
3
When the accelerator pedal is not depressed
4
When the parking brake is not applied




Operation Cancel Conditions
●
Operation is canceled when any of the following conditions is met.
●
When operation is canceled, the buzzer sounds 2 times* and the slip indicator goes off.
Operation Stop Conditions


1
When shift position "P" is selected
2
When the accelerator pedal is depressed
3
When the parking brake is applied
4
When the brake pedal is depressed
5
When several seconds have elapsed after the driver removes his/her foot from the brake pedal
6
When 3 minutes have elapsed without the driver removing his/her foot from the brake pedal


HINT
*
The buzzer does not sound when the accelerator pedal is depressed.
■ NOTICE ■
This function is not intended for parking for long periods like the parking brake. The operation can prevent the vehicle from rolling backward for only approximately 2 seconds after the foot is removed from the brake pedal; perform the normal starting operation within this time.
If it does not operate even when the brake pedal is depressed, release the pressure on the brake pedal once and then depress the brake pedal again. If it still does not operate, check the operating conditions.
The vehicle may not be able to be held stationary on steep slopes or extremely slippery road surfaces.

Effect


•
Since the vehicle is prevented from rolling backward when moving from the brake to the accelerator, the driver can change pedals without hurrying.
•
Since the vehicle is prevented from rolling backward, wheel spin during accelerator operation is reduced, making it easier to start off on slippery road surfaces.

Precautions


•
Hill-start assist control is not a function for holding the vehicle stationary on a slope.
•
When hill-start assist control is operating, the slip indicator light in the combination meter blinks and the brake lights illuminate.
•
If the engine is stopped while hill-start assist control is operating, operation ends without warning the driver.
•
If the brake control of hill-start assist control is operated continuously, the temperature of the brake actuator increases. In this case, hill-start assist control is temporarily suspended to protect the system.
•
Hill-start assist control is a system that prevents the vehicle from rolling backward by controlling the brakes of the 4 wheels, but it may not be fully effective on extremely steep slopes or on road surfaces with extremely low friction such as icy roads.

Impact Safety Body (GOA)

Impact Safety Body (GOA)

●
An impact safety body (GOA) has been adopted that effectively absorbs and disperses collision energy through crushable front and rear body sections and highly rigid cabin sections, minimizing cabin deformation.
●
An all-direction compatibility body structure has been adopted. By using a structure that minimizes cabin deformation in frontal and side collisions, collision safety performance simulating real-world accidents is pursued.
●
Based on computer collision simulations and numerous actual vehicle tests, a rational body structure has been adopted that effectively absorbs and disperses collision energy not only in frontal and side collisions but also in offset frontal collisions in which only one side of the vehicle receives the impact.

Front Energy Absorbing Structure


•
A rational body structure has been adopted that minimizes cabin deformation while reducing the impact on occupants, by means of a framework from the front to the underbody that effectively absorbs and disperses the energy of a frontal collision and a strong cabin framework.
•
The front door inner reinforcement has been strengthened, creating a structure that efficiently disperses the collision load by effectively transmitting energy from the front side members to the roof, door belt line and rocker sections.
•
The front side members and crash boxes use high-tensile steel sheet, creating a structure that efficiently disperses the energy of a collision from the front.
•
An under member has been provided, creating a structure that efficiently disperses the energy from the radiator support.
•
A moment cancel member has been provided, creating a structure that efficiently disperses the collision load from the front side members.
•
A dash cross member has been provided, creating a structure that effectively disperses the collision load from the front side members to the belt line and roof sections.
•
A front side member rear No.1 reinforcement has been provided, ensuring the strength of the tunnel section and creating a structure that efficiently disperses the collision load from the front side members.


Side Energy Absorbing Structure


•
Around the door openings, high-strength framework members including reinforcements have been optimally arranged, creating a rational body structure that effectively absorbs and disperses the energy of a side collision.
•
High-tensile steel sheet with optimized strength distribution is used for the center body pillar outer reinforcement, rocker outer reinforcement and rocker inner panel, creating a structure that minimizes cabin deformation.
•
The roof section has a roof reinforcement, creating a structure that effectively transmits collision energy to the side opposite the collision.
•
The rocker section has bulkheads provided in the rocker cross section, creating a structure that efficiently transmits and disperses collision energy input from the lower part of the center pillar, etc. to each cross member.

•
In the doors, the door side impact protection beams are arranged so that their lower ends overlap each pillar, creating a structure that efficiently absorbs and disperses collision energy. In addition, door strength has been ensured by optimally arranging the reinforcements at the belt line.
•
Energy absorbers for impact absorption have been provided inside the front door trim, creating a structure that reduces the impact on occupants in a collision.
•
A rear seatback hinge bracket and wheel house reinforcement have been provided on the interior side of the rear end of the rocker section, creating a structure that efficiently absorbs and disperses collision energy from the rear door side impact protection beam via the wheel house to the cross member and rear seatback frame.


Head Impact Protection Structure

Head Impact Protection Structure

●
A "head impact protection structure" has been adopted to reduce the impact when an occupant's head, etc. strikes the pillars or roof side sections due to rebound in a collision.
●
The impact-absorbing garnishes with integrally molded ribs collapse, reducing the impact on the occupant's head.

Brake Pedal Retraction Reduction Structure

Brake Pedal Retraction Reduction Structure

●
A "brake pedal retraction reduction structure", which minimizes the rearward movement of the brake pedal by moving the brake pedal downward in a frontal collision, has been adopted to reduce the impact on the driver's lower limbs.
●
When the brake booster is pushed in by the rearward movement of the engine, etc. in a collision, the clip (bolt) securing the brake pedal support to the instrument panel reinforcement separates from the brake pedal support, and the brake pedal support moves rearward and downward along the guide plate. As a result, the brake pedal pad moves forward and downward.

Pedestrian Injury Reduction Body

Pedestrian Injury Reduction Body

●
A "pedestrian injury reduction body", designed to reduce injury to pedestrians in the event of a collision with a pedestrian, has been adopted. Impact absorbing structures are used around the cowl panel and hood panel, creating a body structure that reduces the impact on a pedestrian's head, etc.
●
The hood panel has an inner panel shape (longitudinal frame structure) that readily transmits impact in the longitudinal direction, reducing the impact on the head, etc. in a collision with a pedestrian. In addition, a dent reinforcement has been provided at the hood lock reinforcement to create a collision energy absorbing structure and to ensure sufficient stroke in a collision with a pedestrian. The mounting position of the hood striker has been revised to ensure energy absorbing space for the hood.
●
An open cross-section structure has been adopted for the cowl, and the front wall of the cowl louver has a cantilever structure, increasing the energy absorption efficiency in the expected head impact direction and reducing injury values. In addition, at the wiper pivots, an energy absorbing space is provided between the wipers and the cowl top outer front panel, and the pivots move downward in a collision, reducing the impact on a pedestrian's head.

●
A foam bead energy absorber has been provided in front of the front bumper reinforcement and a resin engine under center cover below the radiator support, creating a structure that prevents a pedestrian from being drawn under the vehicle in a collision and reduces the impact on the lower limbs.
